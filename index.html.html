My Evaluation Criteria

Factual accuracy

Is every claim correct? I check against reliable sources and flag common misconceptions.

Relevance

Does it answer the question that was asked, without drifting?

Clarity & coherence

Is it well organised, easy to follow and free of contradictions?

Completeness

Are key points, steps or caveats missing?

Learner suitability

Are the language, examples and depth right for the learner's age and level?

Instruction following

Did it respect every constraint in the prompt, such as length, format and audience?

My reasoning approach: check facts first, then fit to the learner, then quality of writing. I score each criterion separately and always explain the score in a sentence.

Sample Work 1: AI Tutor Response

An original, made-up example to show my method. It is not client work.

Prompt: "Explain to a Basic Six pupil why the sky is blue."

"The sky is blue because it reflects the colour of the ocean. Water is blue, so the sky copies it."
CriterionScoreReasoning
Factual accuracy1/5Wrong. The sky is blue because air scatters short blue wavelengths of sunlight more than red ones. The ocean reflects the sky, not the other way round.
Learner suitability4/5Short, simple words suited to the age group.
Clarity4/5Easy to read, but it is clear and wrong.
Completeness2/5No mention of sunlight or scattering.

Verdict: Reject. A confident, simple answer that teaches a misconception is worse than no answer. Suggested fix: explain that sunlight contains all colours and air scatters blue light most, using a child-friendly comparison.

Sample Work 2: AI-Generated Lesson Plan

Original, made-up example.

Prompt: "Write a 40-minute Basic Six lesson on adding fractions with unlike denominators."

AI output (summary): Objective: "Students will understand fractions." Activity: teacher lectures for 35 minutes, then a 5-minute worked example. No practice questions and no way to check understanding.

  • Objective (2/5): Too vague to measure. It should say what pupils will be able to do, for example add two fractions with unlike denominators.
  • Activities (2/5): 35 minutes of lecture is too long for this age group. It needs guided practice and pupil work.
  • Assessment (1/5): Nothing checks whether pupils learned the skill. Add 3 to 5 practice questions and an exit check.
  • Instruction following (3/5): Fits the 40 minutes and the topic, but the teaching content is thin.

Verdict: Needs major revision before a teacher could use it.
